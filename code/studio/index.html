WHAT 1500 FRAMES PROVED

  • The win is real, not source-dependent. At ~208 kbps on real 4K: PHOTON-C 21.58 dB vs x264 19.23 dB (+2.35 dB at matched rate). Raw 4K RGB is 5.97 Gbit/s — atto runs 28,700× smaller.
  • Crossover ≈ 500–600 kbps. Below it the architecture wins outright; above it x264's context-adaptive coefficient coding earns its keep (30.24 dB at 622 kbps). This is the documented loss, and the roadmap item.
  • Decoder = encoder, bitwise. The atto stream was re-decoded frame-by-frame through the real Decoder path: 1500/1500 frames md5-identical to the encoder's closed loop.
  • Throughput on this 2-core box. Encode ≈ 1.3 f/s at 4K, decode 4.2 f/s — a proof of life, not a product; the synthesis path is embarrassingly parallel and has no threading yet.

WHAT THE METER SAYS (NO WATT-METER IN A SANDBOX — READ THE METHOD)

  • Encoder side — PHOTON-C is the expensive one. ~16 CPU-seconds per second of 4K video (pure-Python pipeline, no SIMD, no threads) vs x264-slow's ~3.8. The quality table used two-pass x264, which costs ~2× the encode pass shown here.
  • Decoder side — the gap inverts. x264 plays back at 210 f/s on this box; PHOTON-C at 5.7 f/s, because every output frame is synthesised at full resolution — that is the price of sending 0.87 KB per 4K frame. RAM, though, is lower: 0.81 GB vs 1.05 GB.
  • Energy framing. This sandbox has no RAPL/powertop/perf — no hardware watt-meter exists here. What is exact: CPU-time. Estimates below assume 10 W sustained (phone-SoC ↔ hot laptop bracket 5–15 W): encoding one hour of 4K ≈ PHOTON-C ~9.7 kWh vs x264 ~2.2 kWh; decoding ≈ 4.7 vs 0.17 kWh. On any silicon you deploy on, PHOTON-C's number scales down with vectorisation/threads that simply haven't been written yet.
  • What the bits save. The atto stream is 1.3 MB for 50 s of 4K — 28,700× below raw — and network transfer cost dominates device energy for streaming; a codec that cuts the wire 30× while costing a few CPU-seconds is net positive wherever power lives in the radio, not the CPU.

03 — why it works at these rates

Transmit a certificate. Synthesise the detail.

Hybrid codecs spend their whole budget on block residuals — at 0.001 bits per pixel there is nothing left to spend. PHOTON-C instead decomposes each frame into a Laplacian pyramid, sends only the small base (plus at most a couple of low detail bands), and rebuilds the rest from the previous frame's real detail, warped by motion both sides derive independently. Two measurements drove the design:

MEASUREMENT ① · detail is 97–99% temporally predictable

On real footage, consecutive frames' detail bands correlate 0.967–0.999, and an optimally-gained previous band beats zero-prediction by +11.9 to +29.4 dB of MSE. High-frequency content is overwhelmingly static texture that only appears to move — so once a frame has shown the texture, the next frame has no reason to retransmit it.

MEASUREMENT ② · detail is NOT spatially predictable

An upsampled lower band predicts the band above with correlation 0.03–0.13 (worth at best +0.12 dB). Sharpening the base does not invent texture. So where mainstream codecs extrapolate upward, PHOTON-C refuses to pretend: a band must be carried by some frame, and the progressive refresh carries it exactly once per GOP, interleaved into steady-state bitrate.

CONSEQUENCE · what the bits actually buy

At the lowest preset a 4K frame is ~3.5 KB — about the cost of a web favicon — and a whole second of 4K video is under 0.8 MB. The budget goes where it pays: the tiny base and the refresh; everything else is free energy the decoder re-derives from motion + history + seeds.

04 — and how it actually does

The honest scoreboard.

This table is loaded from the benchmark files this box produced. Nothing is smoothed.

WHERE IT WINS

  • 4K at extreme bitrate. At ≈200 kbps PHOTON-C holds 22.4–24.5 dB on the 4K stream where x264 produces 13 dB of block garbage. The architecture is calibrated for exactly this regime.
  • No keyframe spikes. A conventional 4K keyframe is megabytes in one frame; PHOTON-C spreads the same information over the GOP. Steady ~3.5 KB frames make a dial-up-class stream schedulable.
  • Memory. x264, x265 and AV1 were OOM-killed at 4K in this 2 GB environment; PHOTON-C encodes 4K in ~650 MB and decodes in far less.
  • Decode cost. ~0.8 frames/s at 4K, single-core, pure NumPy.

WHERE IT LOSES

  • 50 s real internet 4K (1500 frames). The extreme-rate win survived contact with real footage: at ~208 kbps PHOTON-C decodes real nature 4K at 21.58 dB where x264 crawls at 19.23 dB — the crossover sits near 500–600 kbps, above which x264's CABAC-class entropy coding takes over. All 1500 decoder frames were md5-verified bit-identical to the encoder's closed loop.
  • 1080p real footage. At matched bitrate x264 is ~4 dB ahead and AV1 ~6 dB. PHOTON-C's base-layer entropy coder is a flat rANS pipeline with no context modelling; CABAC is simply much better at that job.
  • Quality ceiling at fixed rate. When detail bands ARE transmitted, the codec is an ordinary hybrid codec with no block-adaptive transform and no complex deblocking filters.
  • High motion chaos. Borrowed texture tracks motion well but occluded or newly-revealed texture needs a refresh frame before it can look right; the refresh rotation is the mechanism that covers it.
  • Things synthesis shouldn't fake. Text, faces at distance, and logos are exactly the content that texture borrowing can't reproduce; they must be sent as NC coefficients.
Straight conclusion: PHOTON-C today is a credible 4K extreme-low-bitrate transport format and a validated proof that detail synthesis replaces transmitted residuals at the far end of the RD curve. It is not — yet — a replacement for x264 across the general 1080p streaming range. That gap is a coefficient-coding problem, the most conventional part of the design, and the least funded part of its 50× smaller codebase.
